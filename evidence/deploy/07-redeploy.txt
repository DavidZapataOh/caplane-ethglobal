Redeployed, because two defects in the registry were immutable and the window to fix them was
about to close.

An eight-agent audit of the workflow, run before the first production deployment, found two
problems that live in the contract rather than in the enclave. Both were unfixable after the first
lien existed, and free to fix before it — the registry held zero liens, so there was nothing to
orphan. That asymmetry is the whole reason this happened now.

All four, not just the registry: the pool and the escrow hold the registry address as a
constructor immutable, so redeploying one alone leaves two contracts pointing at the old one.
Verified after the fact — both read back 0xE7170EE0.


--- defect 1: a paid invoice could never be financed again ------------------------

`_recordLien` refused any lien whose status was not zero. Released is status 2, and the lien id is
deterministic over the claim, so releasing a lien permanently burned the receivable it named.

That is the exact opposite of what releasing is for. The settlement handler's own docblock says it
"closes the lien so the receivable can be financed again", and it could not.

Worse, the two sides disagreed silently: `matchesOf` skips non-active liens, so the enclave would
read `clear`, emit a Record, and the report would revert inside `onReport`. The submitter sees a
mined submission and then nothing from the registry.

The gate now refuses **active and defaulted**, and allows none and released. Default stays terminal
— the pool has already taken the loss against it, and freeing a receivable is what settlement earns,
not what a write-down earns.

  test_Registry_ARereleasedLienCanBeRecordedAgain     record -> release -> record
  test_Registry_ADefaultedLienStaysClosed             default is still terminal
  test_Registry_AnActiveLienIsStillRefused            the gate still gates


--- defect 2: the fuzzy index degraded and could never be reclaimed ----------------

Closing a lien flipped its status and left its three posting-list entries in place. `matchesOf`
walks those lists, so the walk grew with every lien ever recorded rather than with the live ones.
Measured by the audit: 1,467,987 gas for 200 liens after all 200 were released. Monotonic for the
life of the registry. Past the node's call cap every claim against that debtor returns
`undecidable` and is refused, permanently.

Closing now removes the entries, swap-and-pop against a position remembered at record time, so it
is O(1) and cannot itself become the ceiling it removes.

  test_Registry_ClosingALienReclaimsItsPostings   the walk after closing costs under a fifth


--- what that cost, and why the cheaper option was worse ---------------------------

Recording went from 428,571 to 495,743 gas — three extra slots holding each lien's position in its
three posting lists. The budget said 470,000, and the house rule is that exceeding a budget is
justified in writing or reverted. This is the justification.

The alternative was a linear scan at close time, keeping recording cheap. That moves the cost
rather than removing it, and moves it the wrong way: `matchesOf` is a view reached by `eth_call`,
bounded by a node cap and paid by nobody, while `_close` is a real transaction bounded by
10,000,000. Three lists of a thousand liens scan to roughly 6,300,000 gas — so closing would start
failing before querying did, and a lien that cannot be closed is permanently encumbered, which is
defect 1 reintroduced by the fix for defect 2.

A fixed 5% of the transaction budget to remove an unbounded term is the right trade.

The name derives from the string `caplane-registry`, not from the deployment, so the irreversible
check that was made before the first deploy attempt still holds. The deploy script derives it
independently and refuses to broadcast unless the derivation matches what the environment declares
— two derivations that must agree, which is the only defence against a mistake the chain accepts
without complaint.


--- nothing was trapped -------------------------------------------------------------

The old pool held 16 USDC across two holders. Both redeemed before anything was redeployed —
10 USDC to the deployer, 6 to the investor — and both redeposited into the new pool, which reads
16,000,000 base units again. No value was stranded in a contract nobody can reach.

--- what this does not establish -----------------------------------------------------

Zero liens exist. `LienRecorded` has never been emitted by this registry or the previous one, and
the report path from a DON-signed report through the production forwarder into `onReport` has
still never executed anywhere. The interop tests prove the registry decodes the encoder's bytes;
they run against a test forwarder with its own workflow identity.

The remaining audit finding in this contract is not fixed and is declared instead: the fuzzy
collision check runs in the enclave, not on chain, so a hostile RPC endpoint could answer `clear`
to every query. Putting `matchesOf` inside `_recordLien` would make every write scale with debtor
density against the transaction gas limit — the same ceiling, moved onto the path where failure is
worse. The enclave read is pinned to the trigger's own block, which closes the race; the hostile
endpoint remains inside the declared trust base.
