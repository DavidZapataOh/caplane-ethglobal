Three services, verified 2026-09-10. Two answer on a domain; the third deliberately does not.

--- the two that serve ---------------------------------------------------------

$ curl https://api.caplane.xyz/health   ->  200  tls=0  "ok"
$ curl https://api.caplane.xyz/other    ->  404         "not found"
$ curl https://mcp.caplane.xyz/health   ->  200  tls=0  "ok"
$ curl https://mcp.caplane.xyz/other    ->  404         "not found"

The 404 is the check that discriminates. A 200 on /health alone would also be returned by a
platform placeholder page; a 404 carrying "not found" is the responder in this repository.

Each name needed two DNS records, a CNAME and a TXT. Without the TXT the name resolves and
answers 404 — a failure that reads like success at the DNS layer.

--- the one that does not ------------------------------------------------------

The harness has no domain, no port and no healthcheck, and that shape is the point: it is a
worker, not a service. Confirmed running with its log emitting one line per iteration, sixty
seconds apart.

Sixty seconds apart is the measurement that matters, not the fact that it runs. Billing is per
vCPU-minute, so a loop that never yields pins a core and costs roughly ten times the rest of
the deployment put together — and the hard spend limit is destructive, taking down the public
surfaces with it. The interval is `HARNESS_INTERVAL_MS`; if the faucet cannot keep the signer
funded at this rate, raise it rather than topping up more often.

Its restart policy is ALWAYS rather than the default ON_FAILURE with ten retries: a transient
RPC failure in hour 300 would otherwise end the experiment in silence, leaving the dashboard
showing the last good state. Serverless is off for the same reason — nothing would wake it.

--- what is not here -----------------------------------------------------------

No accounting or screening credential is set on any of these services. They are enclave
credentials; a copy on a platform would hand a server of ours the ability to read the invoice.
