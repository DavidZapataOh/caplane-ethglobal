The adversarial run, against the deployed registry. Measured 2026-09-13.

A worker attempts to pledge a receivable that is already pledged, from its own funded address, and
the registry refuses it every time. What follows is read from the chain rather than from the
worker's own account of itself, because the worker is the thing under test.

Each attempt is the same claim sealed again, never the same bytes replayed. The submission id is
`keccak256(sender ‖ ciphertext)` and the inbox refuses an id it has seen, but the envelope carries a
fresh ephemeral key and nonce, so one claim yields a different id every time. What is identical is
the claim; what differs is its wrapping.

--- why a refusal is not counted as a refusal ---------------------------------

The registry publishes reason 1 for two different facts. One is a live lien refusing a second
claim. The other is a registry the enclave could not read: an undecidable collision check is sent
into the same arm deliberately, because neither of them approves. From the chain they are one event.

So a refusal alone is never counted here. An attempt counts only beside two readings taken
separately and after the verdict: the target still encumbered, and the registry answering its own
identity call. Either missing and the attempt is filed as undecidable.

    bounced       10
    undecidable    0
    unexpected     0

Proved in both directions rather than asserted — `services/harness/src/classify.test.ts`:
a refusal with an unreachable registry is undecidable; a refusal against a released target is
undecidable; a refusal for any reason other than 1 is named and not counted; and a *recorded* lien
is reported as an emergency, because a harness that succeeded would be the registry-poisoning
attack run by us, in a loop.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT CLOSE: the read that fails in the bad case is the enclave's, and the one checked
here is the worker's own, from another client moments later. A node-side outage with a healthy
endpoint on this side would still be counted as a bounce. The window is narrowed, not closed.
Closing it would need CRE execution telemetry, which has no public surface a reader could check.

--- what the worker's own journal recorded ------------------------------------

Nine of the ten. The chain holds ten and the worker wrote nine, because a bounded run stopped
before its last write. The gap is in the worker's record, not in the run, and it is why the numbers
above come from the chain. Fixed in `src/loop.ts`: the journal is written after every iteration and
once more on the way out.

--- reformatting, and why it is not on this list ------------------------------

The stated shape of this deliverable was "reformatted fields bouncing". They cannot bounce, and not
for want of trying. The collision check is the last of five, and the four before it pin every
component a submitter could vary:

    debtorTaxId, issuerTaxId, country   overwritten by the enclave from the ledger
    invoiceNumber                       interpolated literally into the ledger query
    amountMinor, currency, dueDate      compared exactly against the invoice, twice — once by the
                                        ledger check and again by the debtor's confirmation

A reformatted claim is therefore refused earlier and for a different reason — SourceUnverified, not
AlreadyEncumbered. On chain this can only ever be seven of seven. The tolerance the index has is
real and is measured where it lives, against the same matcher the enclave imports
(`web/app/[mode]/harness/reformatting.bun-test.ts`, over `evidence/data/02-invoice-corpus.json`):
six real renderings of one invoice all score at or above the threshold of six, and the seventh
entry — a genuinely different receivable — scores far below it.

One tempting shortcut was refused. `evidence/claim/09-rates.txt` records one pair in 903 that
agrees at six: ORC1003 and ORC1008, two real invoices to the same debtor for the same amount on the
same date. Encumbering one and showing the other bounce would be a live six-of-seven — and it would
be the measured false match dressed up as a defence. It is a known cost of the threshold, not a
property worth demonstrating.

--- cost ----------------------------------------------------------------------

    gas per attempt        68,350   (budget 70,000, gated in budgets.json)
    cost per attempt       0.00145 native at 21 gwei
    signer balance         20 native — about 13,700 attempts
    ledger reads           2 per attempt, against a tenant allowance of 5,000 a day
    shipped cadence        one attempt per 900,000 ms — 96 a day, 192 ledger reads, 3.8% of it

The cadence is that division and not a preference. At the sixty seconds the worker scaffold shipped
with, it alone would spend 2,880 ledger reads a day — more than half the allowance a person needs
to pledge a real receivable.

The ledger's remaining daily allowance was read from the response headers before and after the run
(935, then 924). That difference is NOT the harness's consumption: the counter is a rolling window
and other calls were made in between. The honest figure is the derived one above.

--- reproducing it ------------------------------------------------------------

    cd services/harness && npm run typecheck && npm test        # 17 hermetic tests, no credentials
    npm run build && HARNESS_ITERATIONS=1 npm start             # one real attempt

The panel is at app.caplane.xyz/harness. It reads the inbox and the registry directly and names no
service of ours, so it keeps answering while our own indexer is switched off — which is the
demonstration the rest of the system exists to survive.
