Accounting credential, verified live 2026-09-10. Nothing here is a value: only key names,
lengths, granted scopes and sizes.

--- the finding the architecture rests on -------------------------------------

$ POST https://identity.xero.com/connect/token
  grant_type=client_credentials&scope=accounting.invoices.read

  response keys: ["access_token", "expires_in", "scope", "token_type"]
  has refresh_token: false
  scope granted:    accounting.invoices.read
  expires_in:       1800
  token_type:       Bearer
  access_token:     989 chars

No refresh token is issued. That absence is what makes the whole design possible: the enclave
reads the vault and cannot write back, so a credential that rotated on use would strand the
workflow after its first execution. It is measured here, not assumed.

--- least privilege is enforced at token time, not just at consent ------------

The connection holds the write scope, but a token minted with only the read scope carries only
the read scope. Verified by asking for each one separately:

  accounting.invoices          -> granted, scope=accounting.invoices
  accounting.contacts.read     -> granted, scope=accounting.contacts.read
  accounting.transactions      -> DENIED,  invalid_scope

The token that lives inside the enclave is minted with `accounting.invoices.read` and cannot
alter the ledger, even though the credential in the vault could mint a writing one. The vault
credential's ceiling is the set of scopes selected on the app, which is why only three were
selected: read contacts, and read and create invoices.

--- the published schema and the console disagree, and the console wins -------

`accounting.transactions` is what the published OpenAPI declares for /Invoices. The custom
connections console does not offer it, and the identity service rejects it as `invalid_scope`.
The granular scopes (`accounting.invoices`, `accounting.invoices.read`) are the live ones.
Recorded because the spec is the more authoritative-looking source and it is the stale one.

--- the connection ------------------------------------------------------------

$ GET https://api.xero.com/connections
  tenantId:   e1218a28-7437-47ec-bfb5-252092825083
  tenantName: Demo Company (AU)
  tenantType: ORGANISATION

A custom connection reaches exactly one organisation, which is why the tenant id can be read
once and treated as fixed.

--- the canonical claim, read back through an authenticated call --------------

$ GET https://api.xero.com/api.xro/2.0/Invoices/72476883-0c78-4099-9bde-59530764d1b1

  InvoiceNumber:  ORC1043          (assigned by the provider, not chosen by us)
  Status:         AUTHORISED
  AmountDue:      275000.00        JSON number, not a quoted string
  AmountPaid:     0.00
  CurrencyCode:   AUD
  DueDate:        2026-12-31
  Contact:        Bayside Club, tax number "11 000 111 000"

  isUnpaid(invoice) -> true
  response size     -> 4684 bytes, against the enclave's 102400 cap

The bar is that last pair, not a 200. An empty result is also a 200; a specific invoice coming
back AUTHORISED with a balance outstanding is not.

The amount carries tax: the line is 250000.00 and the claim is 275000.00. What is owed is what
the claim is worth, so the corpus records the gross figure.

--- corrected while measuring, 2026-09-10 ---

The line above says the claim records what is owed. The figure was right and the reason was
not: for that invoice the total and the outstanding balance coincide because it is unpaid. A
claim is identified by the invoice's face value, which does not change when it is paid — most
of this ledger's receivables carry a zero balance and are still the same claims they were.
Whether a claim is unpaid is an underwriting question, answered separately by isUnpaid.
