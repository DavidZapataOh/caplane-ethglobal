Inbox cost, measured from transaction receipts on an Osaka node at the 20 Gwei floor.

--- which EVM rules were actually in effect ------------------------------------

No tool prints this, so it was settled by behaviour rather than by configuration. On the node
the measurements ran against:

  gas_limit 16,777,217   rejected: "tx.gas_limit > env.cfg.tx_gas_limit_cap"
  gas_limit 16,777,216   accepted, status 1

That is EIP-7825, so the node runs Osaka rules and the EIP-7623 calldata floor applies. The
figures below are receipts from that node, not estimates.

--- what one submission costs --------------------------------------------------

Arc's gas token is USDC with 18 native decimals, and the base fee sits at its 20 Gwei floor, so
the dollar figure needs no price assumption at all: cost = gas x 2e-8.

  deploy CaplaneInbox                                     232,316 gas    $0.004646
  submit, 275-byte envelope       (388 B calldata)         54,144 gas    $0.001083
  submit, 4,096-byte envelope   (4,196 B calldata)        186,980 gas    $0.003740
  submit refused as duplicate, 4,096-byte envelope        186,980 gas    $0.003740
  cheapest valid submission, empty envelope (100 B)        47,192 gas    $0.000944

Those first and last figures each rose by 209 gas when the submission record grew from a bare
block number to a block number packed beside the submitter's address. The 4,096-byte figure did
not move at all, and could not: it is the calldata floor, which is a function of the calldata
alone, so it absorbs the whole execution cost and any change to it.

The duplicate and the accepted submission cost the same number, not approximately the same one.
Calldata is charged whether the frame reverts or not, and at that size calldata is the whole
bill.

--- the size that matters ------------------------------------------------------

The seven components of a claim serialize to roughly 200 bytes, so a real envelope is about
275: one version byte, one algorithm byte, a 32-byte ephemeral key, a 24-byte nonce, the
ciphertext and a 16-byte Poly1305 tag. The 4,096-byte cap is the ceiling, not the workload, and
budgeting against the ceiling would flatter the design: it is precisely where EIP-7623's
calldata floor absorbs every storage decision and makes them all cost the same.

--- what the floor does, arithmetically ----------------------------------------

The floor is 21,000 + 10 x (zeros + 4 x non-zeros), and it binds when execution gas falls below
the gap between it and the standard calldata charge. Measured on the 4,096-byte submission:

  calldata                4,196 bytes, 62 zero, 4,134 non-zero
  floor                   21,000 + 10 x (62 + 4 x 4,134)  =  186,980
  standard calldata       21,000 + 4 x 62 + 16 x 4,134    =   87,392
  execution                                                    60,360
  charged                 max(186,980, 147,752)           =  186,980

The receipt is the floor to the gas. Execution is absorbed entirely: the 20,000-gas SSTORE that
makes `submittedAt` a permanent record is free at this size. The ciphertext is XChaCha20 output,
so it is uniformly random and has almost no zero bytes — the floor binds by construction of the
envelope rather than by chance.

At 275 bytes the floor does not bind: the gap is 34,300 - 26,320 = 7,980 and execution is about
27,600, so the transaction pays standard calldata plus execution. That is the size the budget is
set against.

--- why no snapshot can answer this --------------------------------------------

`.gas-snapshot` records the test function's own gas, which never includes the intrinsic charge
or the calldata. Measured in-test against the receipts above, it understates by 1.64x at 275
bytes and by 2.81x at 4,096 (66,706 against 186,980).

Worse, and this is the part worth writing down: the snapshot is byte-identical whether or not
`foundry.toml` declares `network = "arc"`. Deleting that line silently removes 39,228 gas of
real cost at maximum size and moves no snapshot line and no test. `evm_version` is not the
switch — cancun and osaka produce the same numbers; the network selector is. What does see it is
the gas report, which under `network = "arc"` reproduces both receipts exactly, 53,935 and
186,980, and reports 147,752 for the larger one without it.

So this contract is budgeted from receipts, and the script that produced them is committed as
`contracts/script/MeasureInbox.s.sol`.

--- what the caps are, and which one binds -------------------------------------

  EIP-7825 per-transaction cap   16,777,216 gas    a max submission is 1.1% of it
  Arc block gas limit           30,000,000 gas     a max submission is 0.6% of it
  Largest ciphertext under the per-transaction cap: about 418,850 bytes

None of those is the constraint. The binding limit is CRE's log trigger at 5,000 bytes, which
the 4,096-byte cap exists to respect. The cap is a budget from another system expressed in
Solidity, and the contract says so where it is declared.

--- what a flood costs ---------------------------------------------------------

The cheapest valid submission is 46,983 gas, $0.00094. The log trigger accepts ten events every
six seconds, so saturating it for a day is 144,000 submissions and $135. That is a price, not a
wall, and the contract has no owner, no fee and no allowlist with which to raise it.

--- permanence, which the logs do not provide ----------------------------------

A reth node in full mode retains receipts for 10,064 blocks. At Arc's half-second blocks that is
about 84 minutes, after which `eth_getLogs` returns an empty array rather than an error — a
silent loss. The public endpoint answers for far older blocks today, but that is an operator
configuration on infrastructure we do not control, not a guarantee in a specification. EIP-7708
makes every value movement emit a log, so chain-wide log volume on Arc pushes operators toward
pruning rather than away from it.

That is why the contract keeps `submittedAt`: the ciphertext may be pruned, the proof that a
submission existed at a given block is contract state and cannot be.
