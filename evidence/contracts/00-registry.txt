Registry cost, measured against the contract in this repository.

--- what these numbers are -----------------------------------------------------

Gas from `arc-forge test --gas-report` and from in-test measurement, under Arc semantics with the
optimizer at 200 runs. The `matchesOf` figures are internal calls inside a test, not remote
`eth_call`s: the transaction base cost is missing, which a remote view does not pay either.

They are taken COLD. A populated registry leaves every slot warm, and a remote call never arrives
warm — measured warm, the same query reports about a quarter of its real cost. Every figure below
follows a `vm.cool` of the registry.

--- onReport, by branch --------------------------------------------------------

   Record      428,571 gas    writes three slots of lien, seven commitments, three posting
                              entries and one nonce — 8.9% of the 4,800,000 budget
   Release      36,288 gas
   Default      36,612 gas
   Reject       36,182 gas    writes no lien state at all: one nonce and one event

Every branch pays the nonce write, which is why the three that change one status byte cost the
same to within one percent.

--- matchesOf, against registry size -------------------------------------------

The whole reason the index holds three components and not four. A doubling bucket takes seven
distinct values across 43 real receivables, and one of them holds 44% of them, so a posting list
keyed on the amount bucket grows with the registry and the walk grows with it.

      43 liens     60,044 gas
   1,000 liens     62,522 gas

Four percent, across a registry twenty-three times larger. That is the property the three-component
index was chosen for, and it is now measured against this contract rather than argued from a
reproduction.

--- where it is NOT flat, which is the honest edge ------------------------------

Flat in registry size is not flat in posting-list density, and nothing bounds density. Two hundred
liens sharing one debtor and one due date — an ordinary factoring relationship, one client with
two hundred invoices — cost:

   5,075,156 gas

That is 17% of the call cap observed on Arc's primary endpoint. It is inside, and it is the number
to watch: the cap is reached somewhere near twelve hundred liens on a single debtor. There is a
test that measures it, so a change that makes it worse fails rather than surprises.

--- the call cap, which Circle does not document -------------------------------

Probed by sending an `eth_call` whose init code is an infinite loop (`0x5b600056`) and reading the
allowance the node reports back:

  rpc.testnet.arc.io              30,000,000   (primary)
  rpc.quicknode.testnet.arc.io    30,000,000
  rpc.drpc.testnet.arc.io         50,000,000
  rpc.blockdaemon.testnet.arc.io  50,000,000

Passing 60,000,000 to the primary is clamped to 30M; 20,000,000 is honoured. Separately,
`eth_estimateGas` refuses above 16,777,216 — the EIP-7825 per-transaction cap that ships with
Osaka, which Arc's documentation does not mention. Note precisely what that cap binds: the
estimate, not the `gas` field a caller submits. It does not constrain this contract, and it will
surprise anyone who reaches for `estimateGas` to size a call.

These caps are read out of error strings, not published, and a provider can change one without
notice. That is why the design rests on the cost being flat rather than on the margin.
