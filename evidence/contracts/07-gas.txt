Gas, measured with --isolate so the figures are what a receipt would say.

--- how these were taken -------------------------------------------------------

`forge test` reports execution gas: no intrinsic charge, no calldata, no EIP-7623 floor. On a
calldata-heavy path that understates by 2.8x. `--isolate` wraps each call as its own transaction
and the numbers below include all three. Two things it does not do: `--gas-report` is unaffected
and stays execution-only for the frames inside a test, and `view` functions are not isolated at
all, because Solidity emits STATICCALL for them.

Cold-state figures use `vm.cool()`. Without it a populated registry leaves every slot warm and
`matchesOf` measures cheaper at a thousand liens than at forty-three, which is nonsense.

The snapshot is written and checked with --isolate on both sides. Checked the other way it exits
1 against untouched code — verified — and a gate that fails for no reason is switched off within
a week. `--diff` is not a gate: against a snapshot whose every number had been falsified to 1 it
still exits 0, also verified. Invariant lines stay out of both snapshots: their line is
(runs, calls, reverts) and `--check --tolerance` exits 1 against one byte-identical to itself.

--- what the numbers settle ----------------------------------------------------

`matchesOf` is flat in registry size: 60,044 gas cold at 43 liens against 62,522 at 1,000, a 4%
difference across a registry twenty-three times larger. That is the claim the three-component
index was chosen for, and it is measured rather than argued.

What is NOT flat is posting-list density. Two hundred liens sharing one debtor and one due date
cost 5,075,156 gas cold — 17% of the node's observed call cap. Registry size is bounded by the
design; density is bounded by nothing, and that is the honest edge of the claim.

The record path spends 458,261 gas of a 4,800,000 budget: 9.5%. The read path spends 0.2% of the
call cap in the ordinary case. Those two numbers are why most of the list below says no.

--- the ceilings no tool enforces ----------------------------------------------

A single test was measured burning 60,822,041 gas under --isolate and passing — 3.6 times the
EIP-7825 per-transaction cap of 2**24 and twice Arc's block limit. Nothing in the toolchain
refuses it. So both ceilings are asserted in Solidity, on the two heaviest paths in the system:
the record branch at 458,261 and a maximum-size submission at the envelope cap.

--- what was optimised, and what it bought -------------------------------------

Two mappings keyed by the same identifier became one struct each, saving a cold slot per lien.
Measured by receipt against the real USDC on a fork of Arc:

    disburse        158,851  ->  136,936     -21,915    -13.8%
    settle          131,753  ->  116,006     -15,747    -12.0%
    pay             105,307  ->  103,556      -1,751
    refund           65,260  ->   63,499      -1,761
    repay            73,070  ->   75,893      +2,823
    writeDown        33,088  ->   34,582      +1,494

The last two rows are the trade, named rather than hidden. `repay` used to zero the principal's
own slot and collect the 4,800-gas refund for freeing it; now the funding block keeps the word
occupied and that refund is gone. Over a lien's whole life the packing is still 19,092 gas ahead
if the advance is repaid and 20,421 if it defaults.

Neither change touches an ABI: `fundedAt`, `principalOf`, `paidFor` and `settled` keep the
signatures their public mappings had. Both carry a test that settles first and then reads the
raw storage word, because the obvious version — check the next slot is empty after a payment —
passes against every layout including the two separate mappings it replaces.

--- what was NOT optimised, with the numbers -----------------------------------

This list exists so nobody reopens these three days before a deadline.

Truncating component commitments to sixteen bytes is the largest win available: the record path
falls about 16.5% and the read path 11.3%, and the ABI never changes. It is refused because the
write path uses 9.5% of its budget and the read path 0.2% of the call cap. There is no pressure
that justifies spending 128 bits of collision margin.

Decoding the report from calldata instead of memory saves at most 2,497 gas — half a percent of
a branch that uses under a tenth of its budget — and requires hand-written offset arithmetic
against a frozen schema. The copy itself is 331 gas.

Caching the query array in memory inside the matching loop is 830 gas WORSE at every registry
size measured. Copying each candidate's stored commitments into memory is worse still, by up to
4.3%. Both read as optimisations and are regressions.

Deduplicating candidates could save about half a million gas in a pathological case that costs
five million, and the memory scan that would do it costs about the same. Transient storage, which
would be cheap, reverts inside a static call.

The figure that justified this elsewhere was wrong and is corrected here. Measured over two
hundred liens reachable through two posting lists, each therefore scored twice: scoring a
candidate cold costs 20,746 gas and the warm repeat costs 4,629. The number written down before
was 900 warm against 18,900 cold — the cold figure was close, the warm one was wrong by a factor
of five. The conclusion does not move, because an O(C-squared) memory scan costs more than 4,629
a candidate long before two hundred of them.

Packing the seven component commitments is impossible: they are full-width hashes.

Replacing the vault's name and symbol with literals saves roughly 33,000 gas at deployment and
frees two slots. Refused on convention: the vault is implemented the way its library documents
it, and the largest contract here sits at 28% of the size limit. There is no size problem.
