Pool cost and behaviour, measured against the USDC that is actually deployed on Arc Testnet,
on a fork of that chain. No test double appears anywhere in this file or in the suite.

--- the premise everything rests on ---------------------------------------------

The token at 0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000 is a Circle proxy whose implementation
is `NativeFiatTokenV2_2`, verified on the explorer. It is not mainnet's FiatTokenV2_2 and it
does not keep a balance mapping:

    function _balanceOf(address a) internal view override returns (uint256) {
        return _from18Decimals(a.balance, DECIMALS_SCALING_FACTOR);   // native balance / 1e12
    }

Asserted against the live token, not believed: a plain value send of 1,500,000,000,000,000,000
wei to an address raised its ERC-20 balance by exactly 1,500,000, with no call to the token at
all. The assertion is by delta rather than by absolute value, because the suite runs against
live testnet state and any address's balance can change between runs.

Measured on the same fork, moving the same 1.5 USDC of value to an account that already exists,
at receipt level:

    plain value send        27,923 gas     no log at the token address
    ERC-20 transfer         51,879 gas     one Transfer log

So the cheaper of the two paths is the one an indexer watching the token never sees. Three
consequences, all designed for rather than discovered:

  a plain send inflates a vault's assets for roughly half what the token charges, and leaves no
  trace at the token;

  balanceOf truncates: up to 1e12 wei of real value is invisible to the six-decimal view, so a
  zero balance does not mean an empty account;

  one movement can be observed twice, a factor of 1e12 apart. Summing both double-counts.

The vault answers the first with two independent measures: it declares no `receive()`, so a
plain send reverts, and it carries a twelve-decimal share offset, so a donation routed through
the ERC-20 is not profitable either. Both are asserted, and both were proven to discriminate by
mutation: adding a `receive()` turns the first test red, and dropping the offset from 12 to 2
turns the second red at 97,907,950 against the 99,999,999 an offset of 12 returns.

--- measured gas, against the real token ---------------------------------------

Two units, because they answer different questions and the difference is large.

Receipt level — intrinsic gas and calldata included, what an account actually pays:

    approve                 60,886 gas
    deposit                112,977 gas
    redeem                  66,816 gas
    investor round trip    240,679 gas     $0.0048 at the 20 Gwei floor

Execution only — comparable with a probe of OpenZeppelin's reference vault:

    approve                 38,942 gas
    deposit                 77,581 gas
    redeem                  52,304 gas
    investor round trip    168,827 gas

The budget for this contract was set from execution-only probes of a reference vault, so the
execution column is the one those numbers can be compared against, and it sits inside every one
of them. Stated plainly so nobody compares across units later: at receipt level `approve` costs
886 gas more than the 60,000 the budget names and `deposit` costs 32,977 more than its 80,000,
and neither figure is a regression — they are the same operations measured with the intrinsic
charge included, which the probes never had.

Going through Circle's proxy and its precompile is what makes the difference from a plain
ERC-20. Budget for the real token, never for a test double.

Other paths, from the suite's own gas report:

    disburse               136,936 gas     was 158,851 before an advance was packed into one word
    repay                   75,893 gas     was 73,070; see below
    writeDown               34,582 gas     was 33,088; same reason
    totalAssets             15,684 gas     it reads the token, which is a proxy call

    deployment           1,608,864 gas     6,888 bytes of runtime, 17,688 of margin

An advance's principal and its funding block were two mappings on the same key and are now one
struct in one word. Disbursement pays one cold slot instead of two and falls 13.8%. The trade is
named rather than hidden: `repay` used to zero the principal's own slot and collect the 4,800-gas
refund for freeing it, and now the funding block keeps the word occupied, so that refund is gone
and repayment rises 2,823. Write-down rises 1,494 for the same reason. Net over a lien's whole
life it is 19,092 gas cheaper if repaid and 20,421 if it defaults.

--- what the demo can actually hold ---------------------------------------------

The faucet dispenses 20 USDC per address per two hours, confirmed at faucet.circle.com. Gas is
irrelevant beside that ceiling: a full investor lifecycle costs about 0.0048 USDC, roughly two
hundredths of a percent of one claim. Principal is the constraint.

Three investor addresses, one claim each, hold 60 USDC and can fund a 60 USDC advance. Anything
above about 100 USDC has to be farmed across several addresses and several hours before the
demonstration; it cannot be assembled while someone is watching.

--- what the invariants prove, and that they prove it ---------------------------

256 runs, 128,000 sequenced calls, zero reverts: no user operation lowers what a share is worth.
The property is asserted at par — 1e6 assets per 1e18 shares, because shares carry twelve more
decimals than the asset, and an empty vault already answers exactly that through OpenZeppelin's
virtual offset.

That the suite has coverage rather than merely a green light was established by mutation: making
`previewRedeem` round up, in the user's favour, drives the price to 999,999 and the invariant
fails. A run that never reached a redemption could not have caught it.
