The submission cycle, driven from a browser against the deployed registry. Measured 2026-09-13.

A page that showed "submitted" would look identical whether the enclave ever answered or not, and a
sealed envelope proves nothing about confidentiality unless the same search that fails against the
chain succeeds against the plaintext. Both controls are below.

Nothing of ours is in that path after the browser broadcasts. The enclave fetched the invoice from
the accounting ledger itself, screened the counterparty, recovered the debtor's signature and read
the collision index — and published one word.

--- one encrypted envelope, and the control that makes "absent" mean something --

    envelope       886 bytes of ciphertext; the event carries 960 bytes of ABI-encoded data
    plaintext      792 bytes, recovered by opening the on-chain bytes with the enclave key

    field          in the plaintext      on chain
    ORC1043        PRESENT               absent
    27500000       PRESENT               absent
    AUD            PRESENT               absent
    2026-12-31     PRESENT               absent

The right-hand column is the claim; the left-hand column is what keeps it from being vacuous. The
same byte search, over the same four needles, finds every one of them inside the opened envelope.
A search that could not find a field it was looking at would have reported "absent" for both.

--- three refusals on the way here --------------------------------------------

The cycle was refused twice before it was recorded, both times as reason 2, DebtorUnconfirmed —
which is one code covering ten conditions, so the chain names the claim and never the condition.
Opening the envelope and running the enclave's own check condition by condition is what told them
apart:

    0xfe04b521…  claimId mismatch. The page derived the identity the debtor signs from its own
                 fields, and the enclave substitutes the ledger's name for the counterparty and the
                 tenant it read through before it recomputes. No submission could ever have matched.
    0x879837c9…  claimId matched; the debtor was the submitter. A creditor cannot confirm their own
                 claim, and the channel used to accept the signature and spend the one-time link
                 before the chain refused it minutes later.

Both are closed, each with a test that fails if the old behaviour returns.
