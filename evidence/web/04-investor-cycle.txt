The investor console, against the deployed pool. Measured 2026-09-13 on Arc Testnet.

Why this file exists: a console that only ever showed zeros would be indistinguishable from one
that reads correctly, right up until the number it shows changes because something real happened.
So what is recorded here is not a screen that rendered — it is a number that moved, and moved back.

  pool          0x4df4c8d722b3a9ebd18b9094c883b5ff83565d7c
  investor      0xb3f85869D8790F1DDC8981306b5bA1c9F76036cC
  asset         0x3600000000000000000000000000000000000000   six decimals
  share         cpUSDC                                        eighteen decimals

--- the cycle, with real transactions -----------------------------------------

  before        shares 6.000000000000000000 cpUSDC
                pool holds 16.160000 USDC

  approve       0.010000 USDC to the pool
  deposit       0.010000 USDC

  after         shares grew, pool holds grew — both asserted, not eyeballed
  redeem        the shares the deposit minted, and only those

  restored      shares 6.000000000000000000 cpUSDC    equal to before, asserted
                pool holds 16.160001 USDC

The last line is the honest remainder: a deposit-and-redeem round trip leaves one unit of the
asset behind in the pool. That is the vault's rounding, which rounds in the vault's favour on both
legs by design, and it is recorded rather than smoothed over — a test that asserted the pool
returned to exactly 16.160000 would have been asserting something untrue about how the contract
works.

--- what the page shows, and why two numbers instead of one ---------------------

  your shares        6.000000000000000000 cpUSDC
  worth              6.060000 USDC
  redeemable now     6.060000 USDC
  pool holds        16.160001 USDC
  out on advances    0.000000 USDC

`worth` and `redeemable now` are equal today only because nothing is lent out. They are different
questions and the page asks both: the pool caps a redemption at what it holds in cash, and once an
advance is outstanding the second number is smaller than the first. Showing only the value would
promise money the pool does not have that day.

--- what is hand-encoded, and what checks it ------------------------------------

No chain library reaches this route's bundle. The three calls are a four-byte selector plus
fixed-width words, written out — and every selector is pinned by a test that derives it from the
real signature with viem, which stays a development dependency:

  deposit(uint256,address)              0x6e553f65
  redeem(uint256,address,address)       0xba087652
  approve(address,uint256)              0x095ea7b3
  allowance(address,address)            0xdd62ed3e
  balanceOf(address)                    0x70a08231
  totalAssets()                         0x01e1d114
  decimals()                            0x313ce567
  maxRedeem(address)                    0xd905777e
  outstandingPrincipal()                0x29b1829e

The encodings are compared byte for byte against `encodeFunctionData` from the same library. A
transcribed selector fails the same way a wrong one does — the call returns empty bytes, they
decode to zero, and the page would report a position of nothing about a wallet that holds shares.
