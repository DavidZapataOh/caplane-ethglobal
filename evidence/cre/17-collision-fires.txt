This is the arm that could not be demonstrated until now. The registry is only ever written by a
DON-signed report, there is no seeding function, and staging carries a workflow name the contract
refuses — so until the first lien existed a few minutes earlier, there was nothing to collide with.


--- what the second submission was --------------------------------------------------

The same claim, sealed again. A fresh ephemeral key gives a different ciphertext and therefore a
different `submissionId`, so the inbox accepted it without complaint — its duplicate guard is keyed
on `keccak(sender ‖ ciphertext)` and saw a value it had never seen.

  first  submissionId  0xdb0577a4e0025f56d6b8e3f71871a42dccb2bc6447aa366685b42d33505752b3  → recorded
  second submissionId  0x9c22eff8f4efee07013d86feaf5f1a559053004f1f61915873690d57e5a08b29  → rejected

So the refusal is the enclave's, not the inbox's. That distinction is the whole point: the chain
cannot tell two sealed claims apart, and the enclave can.

A Reject is a report like any other. It is signed, transmitted and accepted, and it lands as an
event the submitter can read. It is not a silence.


--- the reason code is the evidence, not the rejection ------------------------------

`reason 1` is `AlreadyEncumbered`. A rejection for an expired credential, a missing debtor
confirmation or an unverifiable invoice would have looked identical from outside — a red
submission — and would have proven nothing about the collision index. The code names which check
refused, and this one names only that check.


--- WHAT THIS IS NOT: it is 7 of 7, not 6 of 7 ---------------------------------------

Stated plainly because the distinction is the product's actual claim.

The registry's fuzzy tier fires at six of seven peppered component commitments. What fired here is
**seven of seven** — the identical claim — so the two liens share a `lienId` and the submission
would have been caught by exact-key equality as well. This demonstrates that the check runs and
refuses; it does not isolate the fuzzy tier from the exact one.

Six of seven is not demonstrable against this ledger, and the reason is a direct consequence of
the fix that closed a much worse problem. Three of the seven components — the debtor, the issuer
and the jurisdiction — used to be read straight from the submitter's document and checked against
nothing, so editing any two of them dropped agreement to five, read as `clear`, and recorded a
second lien on the same receivable. They are now bound: the debtor comes from the contact the
ledger returned, the issuer from the credential the enclave used, the jurisdiction from config.

That leaves the submitter in control of the invoice number, the amount and the due date — and all
three are compared against the real invoice. Two different invoices cannot share a number, so a
genuine six-of-seven needs two real invoices agreeing on both amount bucket and due date.

Measured across all fourteen live receivables in the tenant, every pair:

  5/7   ORC1038 vs ORC1032    Marine Systems / Cube Land
  5/7   ORC1033 vs ORC1041    DIISR - Small Business Services / itself, twice
  5/7   ORC1034 vs ORC1040    Pinnacle Management / Bayside Club

Nothing reaches six. That is a good result for false positives — the threshold is not manufacturing
collisions on real data — and it means the fuzzy tier has no natural subject here. The case it was
calibrated for is a receivable re-entered under a different number with the same amount and date,
which this ledger does not contain, and the accounting connection holds read-only scope on
invoices so one cannot be seeded.

The six-of-seven predicate itself is measured off-chain against a real corpus and recorded
separately. What remains unproven is the two meeting: the on-chain index answering six for a claim
that is not byte-identical.


--- the pool has not moved -----------------------------------------------------------

16,000,000 base units, unchanged. Recording a lien commits an advance; it does not pay one.
`disburse` is a separate permissionless call and nobody has made it.
