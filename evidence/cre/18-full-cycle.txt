--- who triggered what, which is the point ------------------------------------------

  disburse   called by 0xbb8449f3…  the DEBTOR's account
  settle     called by 0xb3f85869…  the INVESTOR's account

Neither party had any authority over the money it moved. `disburse` pays the borrower named in the
registry; `settle` splits what the escrow holds. What authorises each is the state on chain, never
the caller. There is no keeper, no operator, and no key to lose — a claim this project has made in
prose since the beginning and which now has transactions behind it.

The two enclave-driven steps needed no caller at all. The lien was written because a log trigger
fired, and released because another one did.


--- the release is the step that had never run anywhere ------------------------------

The second handler — array index 1, subscribed to the escrow — had never executed in any
environment. Its own plan recorded it as NOT DONE, because it needs a real `Settled` event and
producing one needs a recorded lien, a funded pool, a disbursement and a debtor payment. Every one
of those happened for the first time in the last twenty minutes.

  1. trigger                            success
  2. consensus  Report                   success
  3. evm        WriteReport              success

Zero HTTP calls, zero secrets consumed beyond the ring read. The handler reads the lien id out of
`topics[1]` and emits a Release; the whole decision is the event. It does not check the lien's
status first — the registry checks it, and checking in the enclave would cost a chain read, which
is the second crossing the package exists to avoid.

  statusOf   1 → 2


--- and it confirms the contract fix, in production ------------------------------------

The registry was redeployed hours earlier because two defects in it were immutable. This execution
exercises both sides of one of them.

The old `_close` flipped the status and left the lien's three posting-list entries behind, so the
fuzzy lookup walked dead entries for the life of the registry with no way to reclaim them. The new
one removes them, swap-and-pop against a position remembered at record time. A released lien now
costs the index nothing.

The other defect — a released lien could never be recorded again, so a paid invoice was burned
forever — is now testable for the first time on a real lien, and is not tested here: doing it would
mint a second lien on the same receivable and consume more of the pool. It is covered by
`test_Registry_ARereleasedLienCanBeRecordedAgain` against the same bytecode.


--- the money, and where the fee went ---------------------------------------------------

  pool cash        16,000,000 → 8,000,000 → 16,160,000
  totalAssets      16,000,000 → 16,000,000 → 16,160,000
  share price       1,000,000 →  1,000,000 →  1,009,999

During the loan `totalAssets` did not move: cash fell by 8,000,000 and `_outstanding` rose by the
same. Lending is not a loss, and the share price stayed at exactly 1 USDC — depositors are neither
diluted nor enriched by the act of lending.

The 160,000 that arrived on repayment is the fee: 2% of an 8 USDC advance, spread across a 16 USDC
pool, which is the 1.0% the share price moved. The borrower received 8.00 and repaid nothing; the
debtor paid 8.16, which is what the escrow demanded of it against that lien.

The debtor never needed the financing terms to pay. `PROYECTO.md` used to promise the debtor "never
sees the terms of the financing", which is false as a property of the chain — `lienOf` is a public
view. What is true is more modest and is what this transaction shows: the debtor does not need
them.


--- what is still not demonstrated -------------------------------------------------------

**Six of seven.** The collision that fired was seven of seven — the identical claim — so exact-key
equality would have caught it too. Measured across all fourteen live receivables in the tenant, no
two reach six, so the fuzzy tier has no natural subject in this ledger. Recorded separately.

**Default.** No handler produces a `ReportKind.Default`, by construction: none of CRE's three
trigger primitives can detect an overdue lien, and the design that would work is named in the
dossier rather than built. The registry accepts a default at the right second — proven in tests
against this bytecode — and nothing fires one.

**Attestation.** The DON verifies the enclave's attestation before an execution completes. Nothing
in this repository reads that verification, so it is trusted, not checked here.
