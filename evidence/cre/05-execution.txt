Vault-to-enclave round trip, measured by ablation.
=================================================

The handler holds no `runtime.log`, so `cre execution logs` returns `No logs found`, and the
report only materialises when an on-chain receiver consumes it. The verdict is therefore not
readable from outside the enclave — which is the property being built, not a gap in the evidence.

So the proof is not the verdict. It is that the execution has two distinguishable shapes
depending on whether the Vault holds the secret. Same binary, same config, same schedule.

  secret present -> SUCCESS,  trigger -> http-actions.SendRequest -> consensus.Report
  secret absent  -> FAILURE,  trigger, and nothing after it

The HTTP call is never attempted when the secret is missing: `getSecrets(...).result()` aborts
the handler first. That is what makes the green meaningful. A green with no counterfactual would
only show that nothing crashed.

--- what this does and does not establish ------------------------------------

Established: the Vault DON releases a secret into an attested enclave running under
`handlerInTee` on Nitro in us-west-2, and the workflow cannot proceed without it.

Not established: that the credential is valid upstream. `http-actions` reports `success` for the
transport, not for the status code, so a 401 and a 200 produce identical observable output. Proving
credential validity needs a real credential AND an on-chain receiver to read the verdict out of the
report; both arrive later. Keeping the probe credential disposable is deliberate: a shared failure
between "Vault broken" and "credential bad" would be unattributable.
